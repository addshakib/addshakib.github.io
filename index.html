Open to PhD, research, and academic opportunities

I explore how people interact with technology, make decisions, and navigate security and privacy in everyday life.

I’m a master’s student in Computer Science and Engineering at BRAC University in Dhaka. My MSc thesis research, supervised by Dr. S M Taiabul Haque, examines how organizational, technological, and human factors shape the interpretation and implementation of information security policies in Bangladeshi organizations. I’m particularly interested in how employees navigate security requirements in everyday work, and how policy deviations, informal workarounds, and exceptions are understood and managed by employees and security decision-makers.

My interest in the human side of security grew from my bachelor’s thesis, supervised by Dr. Md Sadek Ferdous, which explored an NFT-based authentication mechanism for federated metaverses. Combining technical security analysis with a user study made me interested in how people understand, experience, and make decisions about the security mechanisms they use.

More broadly, I’m interested in human-computer interaction, usable security and privacy, human factors in security, and human-centered technology design. I’m interested in research that connects people's experiences and everyday practices with the design and evaluation of technical systems, using approaches suited to the question, from interviews and usability studies to threat modeling and formal verification.

Accepted for publication

A Secure Authentication Mechanism for Federated Metaverses Using NFTs

BSc thesis, 2024–2025. Department of CSE, BRAC University. Supervisor: Dr. Md Sadek Ferdous . Group thesis with K. E. U. Hawak and M. Jawad.

Federated metaverses create authentication challenges when users need to access interconnected virtual environments. This project investigated an NFT-based authentication mechanism intended to support cross-platform access while addressing security, privacy, and usability considerations. The resulting research paper has been accepted for publication at the IEEE International Conference on Blockchain 2026.

01 Technical Design and Security Verification

Designing and verifying NFT-based authentication

The challenge
Authentication across federated virtual environments requires a mechanism that connects users' identities and access rights without relying entirely on conventional password-based or centralized approaches.
The approach
Contributed to the authentication mechanism and threat model, and formally verified the protocols using ProVerif. Implemented a proof of concept using a Minecraft Paper server, a Next.js and Node.js frontend, and MetaMask wallet integration.
Technical evaluation
Formal protocol verification was used to examine security properties. The working prototype demonstrated the authentication flow in a virtual environment.
My contribution
Contributed to the system design and implementation, threat modeling, and formal verification work as part of the thesis team.
  • NFT-based Authentication
  • Threat Modeling
  • Formal Verification
  • ProVerif
  • MetaMask

02 Usability Study and Evaluation

Evaluating authentication in an immersive VR environment

The research question
How usable is the authentication mechanism in an immersive environment, and what difficulties do users encounter when completing the authentication process?
The approach
Planned and conducted a usability evaluation using a Meta Quest 3. Assessed usability through the System Usability Scale (SUS) and a custom UX questionnaire informed by ISO 9241-11 and Nielsen's heuristics.
What we observed
Some participants found switching between the VR headset and a desktop wallet confusing. This highlighted a usability challenge in coordinating interactions across immersive and conventional interfaces.
Research takeaway
Evaluating security mechanisms requires examining not only their technical properties, but also how people understand and interact with them in practice.

Ongoing research

Negotiating Security in Everyday Organizational Work: How Security Policy Deviations and Exceptions Are Interpreted and Managed in Bangladesh

MSc thesis research, 2026–present. Department of CSE, BRAC University. Supervisor: Dr. S M Taiabul Haque .

Organizational security policies prescribe how employees should handle information, communicate with clients, and protect organizational assets. In everyday work, however, formal requirements may conflict with practical constraints, workplace expectations, and established ways of getting things done. My MSc research investigates how security policy deviations and exceptions emerge in organizational workflows, and how these practices are understood and managed in the Bangladeshi context.

01 · Research problem

Understanding security in everyday work

Rather than treating every departure from a security policy as individual carelessness, this research examines the organizational and practical circumstances in which such deviations occur. It explores how employees navigate formal security requirements alongside everyday work demands, how informal practices develop, and how policy deviations and exceptions are interpreted, negotiated, and managed within organizations.

02 · Work completed so far

Preliminary interviews and literature review

Exploratory interviews
Conducted preliminary exploratory semi-structured interviews with software and IT professionals to understand information-sharing practices, client communication, and security-related decisions in everyday work.
Literature review
Reviewed research on security policy compliance, workplace security practices, usable security, and human factors to develop the study's research framing.
Current stage
The research is currently at the framing and exploratory stage. Building on preliminary work and the literature review, I am refining the research questions and examining how formal security requirements interact with practical constraints, informal workplace norms, and organizational expectations. Further empirical investigation is part of the ongoing MSc thesis.

03 · Research direction

What I aim to investigate

  • Under what circumstances do employees deviate from formal security policies when those policies conflict with practical work requirements?
  • How do informal security practices emerge, evolve, and become accepted within organizational workflows?
  • How do employees and other organizational actors interpret, negotiate, and respond to security policy deviations and exceptions?
  • How do organizational expectations, workplace norms, and contextual factors in Bangladesh shape these practices?
